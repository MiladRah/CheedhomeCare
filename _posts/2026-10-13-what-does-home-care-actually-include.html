--- title: "What Does Home Care Actually Include?" description: "What does home care in Calgary include? A clear guide to non-clinical home support from CHEED Home Care – companionship, personal care, meals, housekeeping, errands, transportation, respite, and more." ---

"Home care" sounds simple, but many families we meet in Calgary are not quite sure what it actually covers. Does it mean a nurse? Someone who cleans? A driver? A companion?

At CHEED Home Care, home care means non-clinical support for daily living – the everyday help that makes it possible for a senior to keep living safely and comfortably in their own home. Here is what that includes.

Companionship and daily routines

Sometimes the most valuable support is simply good company. Our caregivers spend unhurried time with your parent – conversation, a shared cup of tea, a walk, a card game, help with hobbies – and provide a steady, friendly presence through the day. We also help keep daily routines on track, from morning wake-ups to mealtimes and evening wind-downs, so the day keeps its familiar rhythm.

Personal care: bathing, grooming, and dressing

When bathing, grooming, or getting dressed starts to feel difficult or unsafe, a caregiver can help – respectfully and at your parent's pace. That can include help getting in and out of the shower or bath, washing hair, brushing teeth, shaving, and choosing and putting on clothes. The goal is always the same: your parent feels clean, comfortable, and themselves, with their dignity fully intact.

Mobility and walking support

A steady arm and an unhurried pace can make all the difference. Caregivers provide support with moving around the home, getting in and out of chairs, and going for walks – indoors or out – so your parent can stay as active as they safely can, with someone right beside them.

Meal preparation

Good, regular meals are a cornerstone of staying well at home. Caregivers can plan and prepare simple, familiar meals your parent actually enjoys, keep an eye on what is (and is not) in the fridge, and make sure eating well does not depend on your parent feeling up to cooking every day.

Light housekeeping, laundry, and linens

Caregivers help keep the home comfortable and tidy: light cleaning in the kitchen and bathroom, vacuuming, dusting, taking out the garbage, laundry, and fresh bed linens. It is not a deep-cleaning service – it is the regular upkeep that keeps a home feeling like home, without your parent having to manage the bending, lifting, and carrying.

Grocery shopping and errands

From the weekly grocery shop to picking up a prescription or posting a parcel, caregivers can run errands with your parent or on their behalf, so the household keeps running smoothly even when getting out has become harder.

Appointments, community activities, and transportation

Staying connected means still getting places. Caregivers can accompany your parent to medical and personal appointments, faith and community activities, social visits, and outings – and provide transportation, so getting there is not the reason they stay home.

Medication reminders – reminders only

We want to be clear about this one: CHEED caregivers provide medication reminders only. A caregiver can prompt your parent at the right time and help them keep to their routine, but caregivers do not administer medications and do not manage dosages, changes, or schedules. Those decisions always belong to your parent, their doctor, and their pharmacist. If a caregiver notices something that concerns them, they will let the family know so you can follow up with the right professional.

Respite and general family support

Caring for a parent is rewarding, and it is also a lot. Respite care means a caregiver steps in for a few hours – or on a regular schedule – so family caregivers can rest, work, run their own errands, or simply take a breath, knowing their parent is in kind, capable hands. And if the regular caregiver is ever away, CHEED arranges for another caregiver to cover until they are back, so your parent is never left without support.

General wellbeing monitoring

Because a caregiver sees your parent regularly, they get to know what is normal for them – appetite, energy, mood, mobility. If something seems different, the caregiver can raise it with the family early, while it is still small. It is an extra set of eyes and ears, and for many Calgary families, that peace of mind means a great deal.

What home care does not include

To be clear: CHEED Home Care does not provide nursing or medical care. We do not give injections, change dressings, administer medications, or provide medical treatment or advice. Our role is non-clinical support for daily living, working alongside – never replacing – your parent's doctors, nurses, and other health professionals. If your parent needs medical care, we will always encourage you to go through the appropriate health care channels.

Every family's needs look a little different. Some start with a couple of visits a week for meals and housekeeping; others want daily personal care and companionship. Care is built around your parent – their routines, their preferences, and the home they love.
